# CORA: Observation-Constrained Structure-Adaptive Representation Learning for Unsupervised Hyperspectral Image Fusion

## Abstract

Hyperspectral image fusion reconstructs a high-spatial-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI) from a low-spatial-resolution HSI (LR-HSI) and a high-spatial-resolution multispectral image (HR-MSI). The two observations provide complementary spectral and spatial information. Without paired ground-truth HR-HSIs, supervision derived from them faces two limitations. First, homogeneous feature processing with fixed sampling geometry provides limited direct adaptation to curvilinear and non-axis-aligned structures. It also fails to explicitly reflect the distinct representation requirements of the two modalities. Second, cycle constraints commonly assign uniform confidence to self-generated HR-HSI references, although their agreement with real observations varies across spatial locations. To address these issues, we propose CORA, a Curvilinear- and Observation-Reliability-Aware framework for unsupervised multiscale HSI-MSI fusion. Its Curvilinear-Aware Asymmetric Fusion (CAAF) module combines modality-specific 2D and latent channel-spatial 3D processing with a shared interaction branch, while gated axis-conditioned deformation adjusts line-shaped sampling according to local feature responses. Training combines observation-grounded consistency with Observation-Reliability-Guided Dual Pullback (ORDP) and D4 equivariance. ORDP forms fixed spatial and spectral pullback cycles and weights their errors using intensity and structural agreement with the observations. Less reliable regions therefore contribute less to cycle supervision. LR-HSI spectral-shape consistency complements observation fidelity, while D4 equivariance constrains predictions under rotation and reflection. These constraints require no ground-truth HR-HSIs. CORA is evaluated on CAVE, Pavia, and Chikusei under fixed observation protocols to assess spatial, spectral, and structural reconstruction.
